# 04 · Envelope, climate and MEP planning

A house is a shelter first. The envelope keeps water, air, vapor and heat where they belong, and when it fails the result is rot, mold and high bills, problems that stay hidden until they are expensive. This chapter covers what the drawings must show to make the envelope work, how climate shapes the design, and the mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) decisions that change the plan.

## E-LAYERS-1: four control layers

Building Science Corporation (Lstiburek) ranks the control functions:

1. **Water**: rain and groundwater. The water-resistive barrier (WRB), flashing, roofing, below-grade waterproofing and drainage.
2. **Air**: the air barrier. Sheathing with taped seams, gaskets, sealants, and air-sealed drywall.
3. **Vapor**: vapor retarders chosen by climate. They control diffusion and do not stop all vapor.
4. **Thermal**: insulation, continuous wherever possible.

The **"perfect wall"** puts the water, air and vapor control layers on the outside of the structure, insulation outside that, and cladding outermost on a drained gap. The same order works for roofs ("the perfect wall leaned over") and slabs. Wood stays warm and dry inside the control layers.

**Pen test.** In every wall, roof and slab section, and at every transition (foundation to wall, wall to roof, window to wall), draw each control layer as a continuous line without lifting the pen. Each break in a line is a detail that has to be designed.

## E-AIR-1: airtightness

- Code maximum (IECC 2021): **3.0 ACH50** in climate zones 3–8 and **5.0 ACH50** in zones 0–2, verified by blower-door test (✅).
- **Playbook target: ≤ 2.0 ACH50** (Thumb). It is achievable with a planned air barrier and pays back in comfort and durability.
- Airtight houses need mechanical ventilation (M-VENT-1). Build tight and ventilate deliberately.

## E-INSUL-1: insulation and windows

Use the IECC prescriptive table for the project's climate zone (chapter 02, energy baseline, ⚠️ verify). Design notes:

- **Continuous insulation (ci)** outside the studs cuts thermal bridging through framing. In zones 4–8 it is a common prescriptive path, for example R-13+10ci or R-20+5ci. Windows and doors need extension jambs to suit.
- Under an attic, ceiling R-49 to R-60 needs depth at the eaves. Specify raised-heel or energy-heel trusses so full insulation reaches over the top plate.
- Window U-factor and SHGC drive both comfort and code. In hot climates choose low SHGC; in cold climates on the south, higher SHGC can help (E-SUN-1).

## E-VAPOR-1: vapor retarders by climate

IRC R702.7 requires a **Class I or II interior vapor retarder in zones 5–8 and Marine 4**. Class III (latex paint) is allowed where vented cladding or enough continuous exterior insulation keeps the sheathing warm. The table's thresholds changed between editions, so check the adopted one. Never put a Class I retarder such as polyethylene on the interior of a wall that also has low-perm exterior insulation, because the wall then cannot dry in either direction.

## E-FLASH-1: openings

Most leaks happen at openings. The sequence that works (Building America):

1. A sloped or back-dammed **sill pan**, flashing tape or a preformed pan, turned up the jambs.
2. Set the window, with sill shims leaving the pan free to drain.
3. **Jamb flashing**, lapped over the sill pan.
4. **Head flashing** over the jamb flashing, with the WRB lapped over the head flashing.

Everything laps shingle-fashion: upper over lower, so water always runs outward. Show the sequence in a typical window detail at the design development (DD) phase.

## E-RAIN-1: drainage and capillary breaks

- **Rainscreen.** In wet climates, put a drained and ventilated gap, about ⅜–¾ in. (⚠️), behind cladding so the wall can dry.
- **Capillary breaks.** Polyethylene and a gravel layer under slabs, and a break between footing and foundation wall, so ground moisture cannot wick up.
- **Site drainage.** Grade falls 6 in. in 10 ft (S-FDN-1). Gutters discharge away from the foundation.

## E-ATTIC-1: attics

- **Vented attic**: net free vent area of **1/150** of the attic floor, or **1/300** when 40–50% of it is high (ridge) and the rest low (soffit). Keep the attic floor (the ceiling plane) airtight.
- **Unvented attic** (R806.5): the whole attic goes inside the envelope, with air-impermeable insulation (rigid or spray foam) at or above the roof deck. Minimum R-values by climate zone (2021): **R-5** in zones 1–3, **R-10** in 4C, **R-15** in 4A/4B, **R-20** in 5, **R-25** in 6, **R-30** in 7, **R-35** in 8. Use an unvented attic when ducts or equipment must be in the attic (M-DUCT-1).
- **Ice dams** in cold climates: an airtight ceiling, full insulation at the eaves and good venting, plus ice-barrier underlayment at the eaves as code requires.

## E-SUN-1 / E-WWR-1: climate-responsive design

- **Overhangs.** Size south overhangs from the site latitude's solstice sun angles: fully shade south glass at the **summer solstice noon sun** and admit it at the **winter solstice**. No single formula exists, so use a sun-angle calculator or the overhang geometry for the latitude. Overhangs lose effectiveness beyond about **30° off true south**.
- **East and west glass** gets low sun that overhangs cannot stop. Use vertical fins, deep porches, exterior shades, planting, or less glass (P-ORIENT-1).
- **Window-to-wall ratio** (E-WWR-1). Glass is the weakest part of the envelope. Phius CORE's prescriptive path caps window-to-wall at **18%** and skylights at **3%** of the roof, a useful upper bound when energy comes first. Above about 25–30%, model the energy effect before committing.
- **Cross-ventilation.** Put operable windows on opposite or adjacent walls of main rooms, and high and low openings for stack effect in two-story spaces.
- **Thermal mass** (slab floors, masonry) helps most in climates with big day-night temperature swings when it receives winter sun. It helps little in humid climates.

## MEP planning

MEP is engineered later, but its space has to be planned at schematic design or the plan will need surgery.

**M-CORE-1. Wet core.**
- Stack wet rooms vertically and put them back to back (kitchen against laundry, bath against bath).
- Place the water heater centrally. The DOE Zero Energy Ready Home target is **≤ 0.5 gal of water** in the pipe between the heater and any fixture, which roughly means fixtures within about 20–30 ft of piping (⚠️ rough planning figure; depends on pipe size).
- Plumbing walls are 2×6 where a 3 in. vent or a toilet drain runs.

**M-DUCT-1. Ducts inside the envelope.**
- All ducts in conditioned space: floor trusses, dropped soffits, interior chases, a conditioned crawlspace or an unvented attic (E-ATTIC-1). This is a Zero Energy Ready Home requirement.
- Reserve space at schematic design: a mechanical room or closet sized for the system, typically about **30×30 in. service space** in front of an air handler (⚠️), a main trunk route, and vertical chases between floors.

**M-ELEC-1. Electrical panel.**
- Working space **30 in. wide** (or the equipment width), **36 in. deep** and **6½ ft high** in front of the panel (NEC 110.26, ✅).
- Not in a bathroom, a clothes closet, or over stair steps (NEC 240.24, ⚠️).
- Garage, mechanical room or utility wall are typical, close to the service entrance.

**M-DRYER-1. Laundry location.** Dryer exhaust is limited to **35 ft** equivalent length, less **5 ft per 90°** and **2.5 ft per 45°** elbow (IRC M1502.4.5.1). Put the laundry on an exterior wall, or within reach of one.

**M-VENT-1. Ventilation.**
- Bath exhaust: **50 cfm** intermittent or **20 cfm** continuous. Kitchen exhaust: **100 cfm** intermittent or **25 cfm** continuous, ducted outdoors.
- A house testing below **5 ACH50** needs whole-house mechanical ventilation. Plan an exhaust, supply, ERV or HRV system and space for it.
- Outdoor air intakes go ≥ **10 ft** from contaminant sources such as vents, driveways and the dryer exhaust (⚠️).
