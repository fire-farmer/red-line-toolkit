# Starter precedent library

These are houses worth learning from, canonical and vernacular. Each entry names **the move to take**, the transferable idea, and the lens it serves (CR-LENS-1). `tools/deal.py` deals from these tables, so add rows in the same format. They are well-documented works, so look them up for photos and plans. Copy the useful ones into a project's `PRECEDENTS.md` with *your* reason, and add your own. Take the move, never the building (P-STYLE-1).

| House (architect, place) | The move to take | Lenses |
|---|---|---|
| **Fallingwater** (Frank Lloyd Wright, Pennsylvania) | Anchor the house to one site feature (here a waterfall and its rock ledges) and let terraces cantilever toward it. The house is *about* the site. | site, structure |
| **Robie House** (Frank Lloyd Wright, Chicago) | Horizontal layers: long eaves, ribbon windows and a low hearth at the center. Privacy from the street comes from raising the living floor and setting the windows deep. | light, ritual |
| **Gamble House** (Greene & Greene, Pasadena) | Craft as the idea: joinery, sleeping porches, deep eaves. Every detail says how it's made. | material |
| **Villa Savoye** (Le Corbusier, Poissy) | The free plan on columns, the ribbon window, the ramp as a promenade, the roof garden. Movement through the house as the experience. | structure, ritual |
| **Maison de Verre** (Chareau & Bijvoet, Paris) | Light through glass block on a tight urban lot. Movable parts make one room serve many uses. | light, constraint |
| **Villa Mairea** (Alvar Aalto, Noormarkku) | An L wrapped around a garden court, with a forest of poles, mixed materials and soft edges. Modernism made warm. | site, material |
| **Muuratsalo Experimental House** (Alvar Aalto, Finland) | A courtyard with walls of many brick patterns. The house as a testbed: one bold experiment you can live with. | material, contrarian |
| **Eames House** (Charles & Ray Eames, Pacific Palisades) | A kit of off-the-shelf steel parts, set against a row of trees. Industrial parts made domestic. | structure, constraint |
| **Farnsworth House** (Mies van der Rohe, Illinois) | One glass room hovering above a floodplain. The plinth and the floating floor as the whole idea, with a service core freeing the space around it. | site, contrarian |
| **Glass House** (Philip Johnson, Connecticut) | The landscape *is* the wall. One cylinder holds everything private. | site |
| **Casa de Vidro** (Lina Bo Bardi, São Paulo) | A glass living floor lifted on slim columns into the tree canopy. The service wing sits on the ground behind. | site, light |
| **Luis Barragán House** (Mexico City) | Thick walls, saturated color, light falling from above, a hidden garden. The house turns its back to the street and opens to sky and court. | light, ritual |
| **Kings Road House** (Rudolph Schindler, West Hollywood) | Rooms as studios paired with outdoor rooms, sliding canvas walls, sleeping baskets on the roof. Indoors and outdoors as equals. | ritual, climate |
| **Sea Ranch Condominium One** (MLTW, California) | Timber volumes shaped by wind and slope, with roofs that follow the meadow. Several homes form one cluster against the weather. | site, climate |
| **Moore House** (Charles Moore, Orinda) | Two "aedicules" (small four-post canopies) inside a simple box. A room within a room makes the places in an open plan. | ritual, structure |
| **Magney House** (Glenn Murcutt, New South Wales) | One long bar with an asymmetric curved roof tuned to sun, wind and rain. Climate response as the form. | climate, light |
| **Azuma House (Row House in Sumiyoshi)** (Tadao Ando, Osaka) | A narrow concrete box split by an open-air court. Moving between rooms means stepping outside. A hard constraint becomes the poetry. | constraint, contrarian |
| **House NA** (Sou Fujimoto, Tokyo) | A tree of small floor plates at different heights, with no rooms in the usual sense. Section as the idea. | contrarian, ritual |
| **Lunuganga** (Geoffrey Bawa, Sri Lanka) | Building and garden made over decades as one composition of framed views and verandas. | site, climate |
| **Esherick House** (Louis Kahn, Philadelphia) | Thick "servant" walls of bookshelves, closets and windows serving open "served" rooms. A deep window wall as furniture. | light, material |

## Vernacular and modest houses

Ordinary building types carry ideas just as strong, and they fit wood-frame budgets.

| House type (place) | The move to take | Lenses |
|---|---|---|
| **Dogtrot house** (US South) | Two cabins under one roof with an open breezeway between them: a shaded outdoor room that pulls air through the house. | climate, ritual |
| **Shotgun house** (New Orleans) | Rooms in a line, one behind the next, on a narrow lot. Privacy grows with depth, and a deep front porch is the social room. | constraint, ritual |
| **Charleston single house** (South Carolina) | The narrow end faces the street, and long side piazzas (porches) face the prevailing breeze. The entry is through the porch. | climate, site |
| **Saltbox** (New England) | An asymmetric roof, long and low to the north against winter wind, tall to the south for light. | climate |
| **Machiya** (Kyoto) | A deep, narrow townhouse with small courtyard gardens punched through it, so light and air reach every room. | constraint, light |
| **Riad** (Morocco) | Blank walls to the street, everything opening to a planted courtyard with water at its center. | ritual, climate |
| **Longhouse** (many cultures) | One long shared hall under one roof, with zones along its length instead of rooms. | ritual, structure |
| **Queenslander** (Australia) | Raised on stumps, with verandas wrapping the house, lattice screens, and high ceilings that vent heat. | climate, site |
| **Usonian house** (Frank Lloyd Wright, US) | Modest and affordable: a slab floor with radiant heat, a carport, a central workspace kitchen, and an L around a garden. | constraint, ritual |
| **Case Study houses** (Los Angeles, mid-century) | Off-the-shelf steel and plywood, open to the garden. Show how cheap industrial parts can make a generous house. | structure, constraint |
| **Rural Studio 20K House** (Alabama) | A small house designed down to a tight construction budget. Constraint as the design brief, with a generous porch as the payoff. | constraint, material |
| **Moriyama House** (Ryue Nishizawa, Tokyo) | One home split into small separate boxes scattered across a lot, with gardens between them. | contrarian, ritual |

## How to use a precedent in a prompt

Name the precedent and **the move**, then say how this site or owner changes it:

> Take the Azuma House's move (circulation through an open court, so daily life crosses the weather) and apply it to our 40×120 ft lot in climate zone 4A. Keep the court; cover half of it with a deep roof so winter crossings stay dry. One concept statement, key move, section idea, what it gives up.

Never prompt "design a house like X". That gets a costume. Prompt the move.
