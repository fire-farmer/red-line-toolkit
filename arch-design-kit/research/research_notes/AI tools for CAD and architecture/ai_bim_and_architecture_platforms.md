# AI-Assisted Architectural Design and BIM Platforms, and How Claude Can Drive Them (as of November 2026)

> Research method note: All web pages were read through search-engine result summaries. Direct page fetches (WebFetch) were blocked by this session's network policy (autodesk.com, graphisoft.com, chiefarchitect.com and bimsmith.com all failed to resolve). Every claim below comes from a search-result summary of the cited page, not a full read of it. Vendor pricing in particular is often reported inconsistently by third parties; conflicts are flagged inline. Claims older than 2025 are marked **[pre-2025, possibly outdated]**.

## Q1. What does each tool do with AI, what does it output, who is it for, what does it cost, and what platform does it run on?

### Takeaway
By late 2026 every major BIM vendor ships an AI assistant, but most are in "Beta" or "Tech Preview". The working AI features are mostly model Q&A, queries, schedules and tagging, plus image "visualizers". Generative floor-plan tools (Maket, Finch3D, ArkDesign, TestFit, Hypar) produce schematic layouts aimed at multifamily and developer feasibility, with residential-specific options limited to Maket (consumer and small designer) and Higharc (production home builders only). For a solo house designer, the realistic BIM authoring choices are Chief Architect Premier X18, Revit LT or full Revit, Archicad, Vectorworks, or the free Bonsai/IfcOpenShell open path.

#### Open BIM: IFC, IfcOpenShell, Bonsai
- **IFC 4.3 is ISO 16739-1:2024** (approved January 2024, published April 2024). IFC 4.3.2.0 (ADD2) is the Official version, IFC 4.4 is in development, and IFC5 is "Next generation IFC… Currently under development" with no ISO edition — [buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/ifc-4-3-formally-approved-and-published-as-an-iso-standard/); [buildingSMART Technical](https://technical.buildingsmart.org/standards/ifc/ifc-schema-specifications/)
- **IfcOpenShell** (open source, Python) documentation is at 0.8.x and 0.9.0. The high-level `ifcopenshell.api` creates entities (`root.create_entity` for walls, spaces, buildings and types), geometry representations, and openings and door fillings (`feature` module). The docs include full examples covering project, units, contexts, the site/building/storey hierarchy, walls and `model.write` — [IfcOpenShell docs: create_entity](https://docs.ifcopenshell.org/autoapi/ifcopenshell/api/root/create_entity/index.html); [feature module](https://docs.ifcopenshell.org/autoapi/ifcopenshell/api/feature/index.html); [Geometry creation](https://docs.ifcopenshell.org/ifcopenshell-python/geometry_creation.html); [Code examples](https://docs.ifcopenshell.org/ifcopenshell-python/code_examples.html)
- An OSArch post says ordinary scripts (open, by_type, attribute access, `ifcopenshell.api`) need no changes from 0.8 to 0.9 — [OSArch forum](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/26/bonsai-new-release/p15). The post's date is uncertain
- **Bonsai (formerly BlenderBIM)** is a free, open-source native IFC authoring platform on Blender. The current download is v0.9.1 and is labeled **alpha** — [Bonsai Studio](https://bonsaibim.org/studio.html). Blender Extensions listed v0.9.0 on 10 April 2026, and GitHub shows 0.8.5/0.8.6 alpha builds in April and June 2026, so version numbering is inconsistent across channels — [Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/bonsai/versions/); [GitHub releases](https://github.com/IfcOpenShell/IfcOpenShell/releases?q=bonsai&amp=&expanded=true)
- Bonsai generates and annotates 2D drawings (plans, sections, elevations), sheets and schedules linked to the 3D model. Vector section cuts are computed from the IFC file, not the Blender mesh, and all sheets output as SVG (PDF export is also covered). New projects can choose the IFC4x3 schema — [Bonsai Studio](https://bonsaibim.org/studio.html); [OSArch wiki: Bonsai 2D documentation](https://wiki.osarch.org/bonsai-exporting-2d-documentation/); [Bonsai docs](https://docs.bonsaibim.org/reference/general/properties.html)

### Inferences
- **Residential fit, ranked for a solo or small-firm house designer:**
  1. **Chief Architect Premier X18** ($1,995/yr, Win/Mac, residential-native, and now an MCP server).
  2. **Revit LT** (~$540–560/yr) or full Revit (~$3,005/yr), Windows.
  3. **Archicad Studio** (Mac/Win; note that agentic AI and MCP are not in Studio).

## Q2. Does each tool have an API, scripting, plugin SDK or MCP server so Claude can drive it? Official or community? How mature?

### Takeaway
2026 is the year the big vendors shipped official MCP servers. Autodesk (Revit Public MCP Server, Tech Preview, Revit 2027.2+ only), Graphisoft (Public Archicad MCP Server, Beta, Collaborate/Max only) and Chief Architect (experimental MCP in Premier X18 Build 28.2.1.14) all explicitly support local clients such as Claude Desktop and Claude Code. All are preview or beta quality, and official write access is limited or newly added. Community MCP servers (Revit via pyRevit or C# add-ins, Archicad via Tapir, Bonsai/IfcOpenShell) are more capable but unofficial. The most direct, license-free path for Claude to produce real BIM is writing IfcOpenShell Python itself, optionally inside Bonsai through an MCP bridge.

## Q3. What do practitioners say works and doesn't? Are AI floor plans buildable and code-aware? How good is the output?

### Takeaway
The consensus in 2026 coverage is that AI floor-plan generators produce schematic layouts that need significant professional development, and that none produce permit-ready drawings. Vendor "code-compliant" claims are unaudited. LLM + BIM via MCP is good at querying, auditing, schedules, tagging and batch parameter edits. It is unreliable for geometry creation and modification, where AI-generated API code often fails. Official vendor assistants are deliberately conservative (read-mostly), while community servers trade safety for capability.

### Cited Findings
- AI floor-plan generators "produce schematic layouts that require significant professional development before they can be used for building permits or construction". One guide states that "no AI-generated floor plan, as of September 2026, can be submitted for a building permit" — [Illustrarch AI floor plan generators](https://illustrarch.com/interior-design/floor-plans/74667-ai-floor-plan-generator.html); [Zekaiwork](https://zekaiwork.com/best-ai-floor-plan-generators/) (the search summary did not say which of the two made which statement)
- Consumer tools lack "a live, continuously updated database of all municipal codes", and some generators (for example GetFloorPlan) have no code compliance at all — same sources as above
- Research context: a 2023 arXiv paper reports improving the validity of generated floor plans from 6% to 90%. This is a research benchmark, not evidence of buildability — [arXiv 2307.02511 **[pre-2025]**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02511v2)
- Maket: reviewers call it a strong residential option, but its dimensions need manual verification. It is focused on residential and less suited to complex programs, and pricing "can sting" solo users — [Formas.ai](https://www.formas.ai/articles/maket-ai); [ArchPulse](https://www.archpulse.co/blog/ai-floor-plan-generator)
- Snaptrude's AI features are "real but narrowly scoped… not magic wands for instant building design". Its Revit/IFC exports may need cleanup, and it is positioned as complementing Revit rather than replacing it — [Techvernia](https://techvernia.com/pages/reviews/architecture/snaptrude.html); [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/engineering-and-construction/snaptrude-says-its-new-ai-agent-builds-lod-400-bim-models-in-minutes)
- ArkDesign is geared toward typical developer projects and is "less useful for bespoke designs" — [aec+tech](https://www.aecplustech.com/tools/arkdesign-ai)
- Hypar dropped text-to-BIM because it proved "too constraining", which is a signal that prompt-to-building was not production-viable — [ArchPulse](https://www.archpulse.co/tool/hypar)
- **Revit + Claude in practice:**
  - **BIMsmith** observed Claude running five sequential MCP queries (doors, stairs, corridors, rooms, railings) to answer a question, and found that "the questions Claude asks back are often as useful as the answers" — [BIMsmith](https://blog.bimsmith.com/Revit-2027-What-the-Built-In-MCP-Server-Actually-Does-in-Practice)
  - **FetchBIM** onboarded Claude Code plus a skill file as a Revit family creator. A from-scratch test used 21% of the context window, and the agent could read structured error responses, diagnose and patch — [FetchBIM](https://blog.fetchbim.com/i-onboarded-claude-as-a-revit-family-creator)
  - A community revit-mcp listing warns that sending AI-generated code to Revit "may not be successful" — [Glama: revit-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@revit-mcp/revit-mcp/tree/41b6db737dbb4158dcb8699fbe45d7b49bab767f/.claude)
- AEC Magazine's hands-on test succeeded on finishes, ceilings, floor types, a roof and a scope box through pyRevit write access, but one geometry-modification task was "blocked". The next planned test was whether this route can build usable families from shop drawings — [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/bim/giving-ai-actual-write-access-in-revit/)
- **Code-check tools.** No third-party accuracy audit of UpCodes Plan Review exists. One analysis could not verify whether it correctly flags IRC egress, stair geometry or energy code on custom homes — [AI Home Building](https://aihomebuilding.com/stories/ai-native-qa-qc-upcodes-plan-review-residential-gap). Municipal AI plan review keeps humans in the loop. Seattle noted that prescreening saves an applicant a full review cycle only if it catches every correction a staff reviewer would — [Smart Cities Dive](https://www.smartcitiesdive.com/news/ai-building-permits-austin-honolulu-los-angeles/751159/); [Crossbeam](https://www.crossbeam-permits.com/ai-plan-review-buyers-guide)
- Swapp CD outputs "still require professional review before permitting, construction, or regulatory use" — [Futurepedia](https://www.futurepedia.io/tool/swapp)

### Inferences
- For houses, the most reliable division of labor is this:
  - AI tools for **program and adjacency exploration** (Maket, Hypar bubble mode).
  - **Parcel and zoning envelopes** (Deepblocks or Zoneomics where covered).
  - **Model QA and automation** (MCP queries, schedules, tagging, parameter batches).
  - The designer, or Claude writing explicit, dimensioned IfcOpenShell or Revit API code, for buildable geometry.
  - A human code review against the adopted IRC or local amendments.
- Claude's value in BIM is highest where outcomes are verifiable: parameter audits, schedule generation, room/door/stair checks against dimensional rules, IFC generation from explicit specs. It is lowest where geometric judgment is needed without feedback.

### Gaps
- No Reddit (r/architecture, r/Revit, r/BIM) threads surfaced in searches, so first-hand forum sentiment is missing. Practitioner evidence here comes from blogs and trade press.
- No independent residential accuracy test of Maket, Archistar, CivCheck or UpCodes against the IRC was found.
- The final verdict of AEC Magazine's Claude + MCP test was not available from the excerpts.

## Q5. What are the 2025–2026 trends: agentic BIM, MCP adoption in AEC, and Autodesk, Graphisoft/Nemetschek and Trimble AI strategies?

### Takeaway
MCP became the de facto bridge between LLMs and AEC software in 2026. Autodesk (Revit Public MCP, APS MCP samples, Autodesk Assistant built on MCP), Graphisoft (Public Archicad MCP), Chief Architect (X18 MCP), Trimble (SketchUp–Claude connector), Qonic and Revizto all shipped MCP endpoints, and Claude is the most-named client. Vendors are moving from chat assistants to "agentic" assistants, but nearly everything is labeled Beta, Tech Preview or Experimental. Critics argue that MCP does not solve delegated authority, audit or governance for agents acting on BIM models.

### Inferences
- The industry pattern is "LLM orchestrates, deterministic BIM engine computes". For a Claude-centric toolkit, the robust design is the same: Claude plans and writes explicit API or IfcOpenShell calls, then the BIM engine generates geometry and drawings, and validation runs (schedules, clearance checks, IFC validation) before a human approves.
- Vendor MCP servers are converging on local STDIO plus tiered read, trusted-write and experimental toolsets. Expect official write tools to remain conservative through 2027, so community bridges (pyRevit, Tapir, Bonsai) will stay the power-user route.
- Because Claude is the most-cited MCP client across Autodesk, Graphisoft, Chief Architect, Trimble and Revizto docs, Claude Desktop or Claude Code is a well-supported choice for a small practice.
